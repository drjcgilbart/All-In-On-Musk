# Today in Musk World
*Unofficial Musk news desk. Inside Elon's World of Companies.*

**Monday, 16 Nov 2026 · Issue 005 · Times ET**

## The brief

- Musk walked Ship 42's heat shield Sunday and says the caught ship "could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment." Hours later he laid out Starship V4: about 70 m long, nine engines.
- ULA's Vulcan failed about 10 minutes into flight Sunday and lost 40 Amazon Leo satellites. Leo is Starlink's main U.S. rival. It's ULA's first outright mission failure.
- Zelenskyy publicly asked the U.S. and Musk to lift Starshield speed caps over the front. SpaceX has said nothing has changed.

**How we label:** `Confirmed` On the record: the company, a regulator, a filing, or the person's own post. `Reported` A named outlet's reporting that the company hasn't confirmed. `Rumor` Secondhand, unattributed or unverified. Not a done deal.

## The stack: Inside the companies

### 01. Ship 42 checks out. V4 gets bigger.
`Confirmed` · SpaceX · Starship · 15 Nov 2026

A day after the first-ever Starship catch, Musk was at the pad going over the ship. "I was going over the whole Starship heat shield earlier today. Overall, it looks great. The engines look great too. Ship 42 could probably be reflown with a few weeks of refurbishment," he posted at 5:20 PM ET. He added that "many small fixes are needed to achieve immediate reusability (like an airplane)," and that the ship "brings back a motherlode of data for further improvements to the heat shield."

At 8:33 PM ET he moved on to the next ship. "Starship V4 will be ~70m long, which is roughly a 40% length increase and allows >60% more propellant load. Engine count will be raised to 9, with 3 more Raptor-Vacuum engines added, and these will be higher thrust V4 engines." Today's V3 ship stands about 52 m with six Raptors.

- **Context:** SpaceX has never reflown a ship. Its two booster reflights each came months apart, per Space.com.
- **Why V4 matters:** more propellant per tanker flight means fewer launches to fill an orbital depot before a Moon landing. Musk has put that count at 6 to 8. Outside estimates run as high as 22.
- **Still open:** the cause of Booster 22's early boostback cutoff on Friday. SpaceX says the root cause is under investigation.

> Read it as: "a few weeks" is Musk's estimate, not a schedule. No Flight 16 date has been set.

Sources: [Musk on X: heat shield](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2122092374016806969) · [Musk on X: V4](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2122141019894383027) · [Musk on X: heat shield data](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2122108594183414081) · [Teslarati](https://www.teslarati.com/elon-musk-spacex-starship-v4-moon/) · [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/whats-next-for-spacexs-starship-after-epic-flight-15-chopsticks-catch) · [SpaceX: Flight 15](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15/)

### 02. Musk to a critic: explain the chopsticks.
`Confirmed` · SpaceX · 15 Nov 2026

Writer Matt Stoller argued on X that SpaceX's success comes down to capital and engineering talent, not genius. Musk answered at 6:47 PM ET Sunday: "Let's take this gentleman's argument at face value that anyone can throw money at rockets and get something like Starship, Falcon or Dragon." He asked how SpaceX can "catch giant starships from orbit with chopsticks and deliver 85% of all Earth payload to orbit with Falcon."

Per Benzinga's read of the full post, he also pointed at Boeing's Starliner and said ULA accounts for less than 5% of SpaceX's orbital payload. "I am genuinely curious about these questions," he wrote. The post landed about six hours after ULA's Vulcan failed off Cape Canaveral (see Beyond the Stack).

> The 85% and under-5% figures are Musk's own numbers, not an independent count.

Sources: [Musk on X](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2122114284779925897) · [Benzinga via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/elon-musk-takes-aim-boeing-024147342.html)

### 03. Zelenskyy takes the Starshield fight public.
`Confirmed / Reported` · Starlink · Starshield · Policy · 15–16 Nov 2026

**Confirmed, his own post:** Ukraine's president asked the U.S. and Musk on X to lift restrictions on Ukraine's access. "Our interceptor drones lose connection with their operators at a speed of 161 kilometers per hour and simply cannot catch up with Russian jet-powered 'shaheds,'" he wrote. "It is important to be on the right side of history."

**Reported:** Euronews says Zelenskyy confirmed Friday the caps were still in place "along the entire front line" and over Russian-occupied territory. SpaceX told Ars Technica last week, "This is false. There has been no change." Musk has said Starshield limits are set by the U.S. military.

Sources: [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/16/zelenskyy-urges-us-and-musk-to-restore-starlink-access-after-wave-of-russian-strikes) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/ukraine-says-spacex-starshield-is-restricted-again-spacex-claims-it-isnt/)

### 05. "7x safer" goes up in Times Square.
`Reported` · Tesla · FSD · 14–15 Nov 2026

Tesla's first Times Square billboard for FSD (Supervised) reads "7x safer than a human when engaged. 15+ billion miles driven globally," according to Sawyer Merritt, who says it runs through the end of November. EVXL reports it was up by 14 Nov.

The 7x matches the minor-collision line in Tesla's own safety report: about 1.76 million miles between minor collisions with FSD engaged versus an estimated U.S. average of about 225,000. Researchers Reuters spoke to in May called Tesla's comparisons misleading, citing differences such as vehicle age.

### 01. Vulcan fails. 40 Amazon Leo satellites lost.
`Confirmed` · Space · Broadband · Cape Canaveral · 15 Nov 2026

ULA's Vulcan lifted off from Cape Canaveral's pad 41 at 12:40 PM ET Sunday, after a hold at T-20 seconds. ULA said the rocket "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit." Spaceflight Now says the problem came during the Centaur upper stage burn and calls it ULA's first outright failure since the company formed in 2006. Amazon said there is "risk with any launch" and that it still backs ULA. Why it's here: Leo is Starlink's main U.S. rival, and this was the first of 38 Vulcans Amazon bought. Leo has also booked 13 launches on SpaceX's Falcon 9.

Sources: [Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/11/15/ula-suffers-first-total-mission-failure-with-loss-of-vulcan-rocket-carrying-40-amazon-leo-satellites/) · [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/vulcan-launch-of-amazon-leo-satellites-fails/) · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/science/1034006/vulcan-rocket-fails-destroying-40-amazon-leo-satellites)

### 02. Musk signs the "bring Jensen back" letter.
`Reported` · AI · Nvidia · 15–16 Nov 2026

AFP reports Musk is among more than 158,000 signers of an open letter asking Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang, who resigned Thursday, while an independent investigation runs. Other signers include Travis Kalanick, Tim Sweeney and Garry Tan. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Sunday that Nvidia should look "outside" for its next chief. Why it's here: Nvidia GPUs power SpaceX's AI compute, and Musk had publicly backed Huang before the resignation.

Sources: [AFP](https://www.afp.com/en/musk-uber-co-founder-join-call-jensen-huangs-return-nvidia)
